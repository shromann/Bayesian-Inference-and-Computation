Mathematical Tricks for Bayesian Inference & Computation

How to use this sheet

The course repeatedly uses a small set of moves. If you can spot the pattern, most derivations become bookkeeping.

1. Identify the object

Is it a posterior, marginal likelihood, prior predictive, acceptance probability, importance weight, Bayes factor, or full conditional? Write the object before manipulating it.

2. Keep only what matters

Drop factors constant in the parameter: \(\pi(\theta\mid x)\propto L(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta)\). In ratios, common constants cancel.

3. Recognize a known shape

Beta kernel, Gamma kernel, normal quadratic, sum of squares, density-integrates-to-one, detailed balance, or self-normalized weights.

Rule of thumb
If the answer should be a distribution, get a kernel and recognize it. If the answer should be a probability/expectation/constant, either integrate a known kernel or write a ratio where unknown constants cancel.

1. Algebra & Manipulation Tricks core

These are the low-level moves behind almost every posterior, MCMC ratio, and model-comparison calculation.

1.1 Drop constants with respect to the parameter

\[ \pi(\theta\mid x)=\frac{L(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta)}{m(x)} \propto L(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta), \qquad m(x)=\int_\Theta L(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta)\,\d\theta. \]

Anything not depending on \(\theta\) can be absorbed into the proportionality constant: normalizing constants, \(\prod x_i!\) in Poisson likelihoods, \( (2\pi)^{-n/2}\) in normals when \(\sigma^2\) is known, etc.

Use when
You only need the posterior shape, a Gibbs full conditional, an MH ratio, or an importance weight up to a constant.

1.2 Work on the log scale

\[ \ell(\theta)=\log L(x\mid\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^n \log f(x_i\mid\theta), \qquad \log\frac{A}{B}=\log A-\log B. \]

Products become sums; tiny/huge values become numerically stable. In MH, compute \(\log \alpha = \min(0,\log\pi(y)-\log\pi(x)+\log q(x\mid y)-\log q(y\mid x))\).

1.3 Complete the square for normal kernels exam

\[ A\theta^2 - 2B\theta + C = A\left(\theta-\frac{B}{A}\right)^2 + \left(C-\frac{B^2}{A}\right), \qquad A>0. \]

Equivalent normal form: \(\exp\{-\frac{1}{2\tau}(\theta-m)^2\}\) means \(\theta\sim \N(m,\tau)\). This is the standard move for Normal-Normal updates, regression posteriors, and Gibbs conditionals.

Normal-Normal template

Data: \(y_i\mid\mu\sim\N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), known \(\sigma^2\). Prior: \(\mu\sim\N(m_0,s_0^2)\). Then

\[ \pi(\mu\mid y)\propto \exp\!\left\{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\mu)^2 - \frac{1}{2s_0^2}(\mu-m_0)^2\right\}. \]

Expand the \(\mu^2\) and \(\mu\) terms, complete the square, and read off the posterior mean/variance.

1.4 Sum-of-squares decomposition

\[ \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i-\mu)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i-\bar y)^2 + n(\bar y-\mu)^2 = (n-1)s^2 + n(\bar y-\mu)^2. \]

This separates data variability from the parameter \(\mu\). It is essential for the normal model with unknown mean and variance and appears in hierarchical/regression calculations.

1.5 Exponent bookkeeping / kernel matching

After multiplying likelihood and prior, collect powers of \(\theta\) and exponentials:

\[ \theta^{p-1}(1-\theta)^{q-1} \;\Rightarrow\; \Be(p,q), \qquad \theta^{a-1}e^{-b\theta} \;\Rightarrow\; \Ga(a,b), \qquad e^{-\frac{1}{2\tau}(\theta-m)^2}\;\Rightarrow\;\N(m,\tau). \]
Careful
Match the parameterization of the distribution on the formula sheet. For Gamma, confirm whether \(b\) is rate or scale; for Normal, confirm variance vs precision.

1.6 Convex combination / shrinkage form

\[ \E[\theta\mid y] = \lambda\,\E[\theta] + (1-\lambda)\,\hat\theta_{\text{data}}. \]

Example: Beta-Binomial with \(\theta\sim\Be(\alpha,\beta)\), \(y\mid\theta\sim\Bin(n,\theta)\), posterior mean \(m=\frac{\alpha+y}{\alpha+\beta+n}\) can be written as

\[ m=\lambda\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta} + (1-\lambda)\frac{y}{n}, \qquad \lambda=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha+\beta+n}\in(0,1). \]

This immediately proves the posterior mean lies between the prior mean and the sample proportion.

2. Integration & Kernel Tricks high yield

Many Bayesian integrals are not solved by brute force. You rewrite them as a known density times a constant, then use the fact that densities integrate to 1.

2.1 The density-normalization trick

\[ \int_\Theta c\,p(\theta)\,\d\theta = c, \qquad \text{if } p(\theta)\text{ is a density over }\Theta. \]

Equivalently, if you can identify a kernel \(k(\theta)\) of a known density \(p(\theta)=k(\theta)/Z\), then \(\int k(\theta)\,\d\theta=Z\).

Kernel you seeKnown densityIntegral / constant
\(\theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1},\; \theta\in(0,1)\) \(\Be(a,b)\) \(\displaystyle \int_0^1 \theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1}\,\d\theta = B(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}\)
\(\theta^{a-1}e^{-b\theta},\; \theta>0\) \(\Ga(a,b)\) (rate \(b\)) \(\displaystyle \int_0^\infty \theta^{a-1}e^{-b\theta}\,\d\theta = \frac{\Gamma(a)}{b^a}\)
\(\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\theta-m)^2}{2v}\right),\; \theta\in\R\) \(\N(m,v)\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\theta-m)^2}{2v}\right)\d\theta = \sqrt{2\pi v}\)

2.2 Prior predictive / marginal likelihood integrals

\[ p(y)=\int_\Theta p(y\mid\theta)\pi(\theta)\,\d\theta, \qquad m(x)=\int_\Theta L(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta)\,\d\theta. \]

Same mathematical shape. The prior predictive averages the data model over the prior; the marginal likelihood averages the likelihood over the prior. Bayes factors are ratios of marginal likelihoods.

Sample exam pattern: Uniform-Binomial prior predictive

For \(y\mid\theta\sim\Bin(n,\theta)\), \(\theta\sim\Unif(0,1)\),

\[ \P(y=k)=\int_0^1 \binom{n}{k}\theta^k(1-\theta)^{n-k}\,\d\theta = \binom{n}{k}B(k+1,n-k+1)=\frac{1}{n+1}. \]

The Beta integral is the whole trick.

2.3 Marginalize by integrating out nuisance parameters

\[ \pi(\theta_1\mid x)=\int \pi(\theta_1,\theta_2\mid x)\,\d\theta_2. \]

Use when asked for a marginal posterior or when a parameter is a nuisance. Common examples: integrate out \(\sigma^2\) in the normal model to get a \(t\) marginal for \(\mu\); integrate out latent indicators in mixture models to get the observed-data likelihood.

2.4 Change of variables / Jacobian careful

\[ \phi=h(\theta),\; h\text{ monotone} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \pi_\phi(\phi)=\pi_\theta(h^{-1}(\phi))\left\lvert \frac{\d}{\d\phi}h^{-1}(\phi)\right\rvert. \]
TransformJacobian factorTypical use
\(\tau=1/\sigma^2\)\(\left\lvert\d\sigma^2/\d\tau\right\rvert=\tau^{-2}\)Gamma precision \(\leftrightarrow\) Inverse-Gamma variance
\(\phi=\log\theta,\;\theta>0\)\(\theta=d e^\phi/d\phi\)Log-scale random walk proposals / stable parameterization
\(\phi=\operatorname{logit}(\theta)\)\(\theta(1-\theta)\)Unconstrained sampling for probabilities
Jeffreys connection
Jeffreys prior is invariant under one-to-one reparameterization because \(I(\phi)=I(\theta)(\d\theta/\d\phi)^2\), so \(|I(\phi)|^{1/2}=|I(\theta)|^{1/2}|\d\theta/\d\phi|\).

2.5 Complete a normal/Gamma inside an integral

When marginalizing, don’t expand everything blindly. Pull out factors independent of the integration variable, complete the square (normal) or collect exponents (Gamma), then replace the integral by its known normalizing constant.

\[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \exp\!\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left[A\mu^2-2B\mu\right]\right\}\d\mu = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{A}}\exp\!\left\{\frac{B^2}{2A}\right\}. \]

3. Conjugate Updates & Kernel Recognition core

Conjugacy is the "recognize the kernel, read off parameters" trick. It saves you from computing \(m(x)\) directly.

3.1 The conjugate-update template

  1. Write \(L(x\mid\theta)\) and keep only \(\theta\)-dependent factors.
  2. Write \(\pi(\theta)\) as a kernel.
  3. Multiply and collect exponents / quadratic terms.
  4. Match to a known family and state the normalizing constant only if asked.

3.2 Common conjugate pairs

LikelihoodPriorPosteriorKernel move
\(y\mid\theta\sim\Bin(n,\theta)\) \(\theta\sim\Be(\alpha,\beta)\) \(\Be(\alpha+y,\;\beta+n-y)\) \(\theta^y(1-\theta)^{n-y}\theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1}\)
\(x_i\mid\theta\sim\Pois(\theta)\) \(\theta\sim\Ga(\alpha,\beta)\) \(\Ga(\alpha+\sum x_i,\;\beta+n)\) \(e^{-n\theta}\theta^{\sum x_i}\theta^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta\theta}\)

\(x_i\mid\mu,\sigma^2\sim\N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), \(\mu\) known \(\sigma^2\sim\InvGam(\alpha,\beta)\) \(\InvGam(\alpha+n/2,\;\beta+\frac12\sum(x_i-\mu)^2)\) Collect powers of \(\sigma^2\) and \(1/\sigma^2\) in exponent

Beta-Binomial derivation (exam template)

Let \(y\mid\theta\sim\Bin(n,\theta)\), \(\pi(\theta)=\Be(\alpha,\beta)\). Then

\[ \begin{aligned} \pi(\theta\mid y) &\propto \binom{n}{y}\theta^y(1-\theta)^{n-y}\cdot \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1} \\ &\propto \theta^{\alpha+y-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta+n-y-1}. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore \(\theta\mid y\sim\Be(\alpha+y,\beta+n-y)\). The posterior mean is \(\frac{\alpha+y}{\alpha+\beta+n}\).

3.3 Normal unknown mean and variance: the two key decompositions

\[ \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i-\mu)^2 = (n-1)s^2+n(\bar y-\mu)^2, \qquad s^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\bar y)^2. \]

With prior \(\pi(\mu,\sigma^2)\propto 1/\sigma^2\), the joint posterior factorizes as

\[ \mu\mid\sigma^2,y\sim \N\!\left(\bar y,\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right), \qquad \sigma^2\mid y\sim \operatorname{Scaled\text{-}Inv\text{-}}\chi^2(n-1,s^2). \]

Integrating out \(\sigma^2\) gives \(\frac{\mu-\bar y}{s/\sqrt{n}}\mid y \sim t_{n-1}\). The trick is: complete square in \(\mu\), then recognize a Gamma/Inv-\(\chi^2\) integral in \(\sigma^2\).

Posterior predictive
For conjugate models, you can often compute \(p(\tilde y\mid y)=\int p(\tilde y\mid\theta)\pi(\theta\mid y)\d\theta\) by the same kernel-recognition trick. Prior predictive uses \(\pi(\theta)\); posterior predictive uses \(\pi(\theta\mid y)\).

4. Bayes Factors & Model Comparison exam

Model comparison questions usually ask for a definition, a marginal-likelihood integral, or a sensitivity/improper-prior argument. Keep the algebra clean and state what cancels.

4.1 Marginal likelihood and Bayes factor

\[ m_j(x)=p(x\mid M_j)=\int_{\Theta_j} f_j(x\mid \theta_j)\pi_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j, \qquad B_{12}=\frac{m_1(x)}{m_2(x)}. \]

Posterior odds: \(\frac{P(M_1\mid x)}{P(M_2\mid x)}=B_{12}\frac{P(M_1)}{P(M_2)}\). The Bayes factor is the evidence from data; prior model probabilities are separate.

Interpretation scale (common)
\(B_{12}>1\) supports \(M_1\). Rough Jeffreys-style intuition: 1-3 weak, 3-10 moderate, 10-30 strong, >30 very strong (use the scale given in your course if specified).

4.2 Prior sensitivity trick

With strong data, posteriors within a fixed model may be stable, but Bayes factors can still be sensitive because \(m_j(x)=\int f_j\pi_j\) averages the likelihood over the whole prior. Making a prior very diffuse can heavily penalize a model by spreading mass over unlikely parameter values.

Improper priors pitfall
If \(\pi(\theta)=c g(\theta)\) is improper, \(c\) is arbitrary. A Bayes factor involving an arbitrary constant is not well-defined. This is why fractional/partial/intrinsic Bayes factors exist.

4.3 Fractional Bayes factor proof pattern

\[ B_{12}^{F}(b)=\frac{q_1(x\mid b)}{q_2(x\mid b)}, \qquad q_j(x\mid b)=\frac{\int f_j(x\mid\theta_j)\pi_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j}{\int f_j(x\mid\theta_j)^b\pi_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j},\quad b\in(0,1]. \]

Idea: use fraction \(b\) of the likelihood to "train" the improper prior into a proper posterior-like object, then use the remaining \(1-b\) information for comparison. If \(\pi_j=c_j g_j\), the arbitrary \(c_j\) appears in numerator and denominator of \(q_j\) and cancels.

Why the constant cancels \[ q_j(x\mid b)=\frac{\int f_j(x\mid\theta_j)c_j g_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j}{\int f_j(x\mid\theta_j)^b c_j g_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j}=\frac{\int f_j(x\mid\theta_j)g_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j}{\int f_j(x\mid\theta_j)^b g_j(\theta_j)\,\d\theta_j}. \]

The same \(c_j\) cancels inside model \(j\). This is the key exam sentence.

4.4 Exam checklist for Bayes-factor questions

  • Define \(m_j(x)\) explicitly as an integral over the parameter space.
  • State the prior (including whether it is proper/improper).
  • If improper, look for arbitrary constant \(c\) and explain why ordinary BF is problematic.
  • For fractional BF, write both full and training integrals; show cancellation.
  • If asked for large \(n\), look at likelihood concentration / marginal-likelihood behavior; be careful not to confuse posterior consistency with BF prior sensitivity.

5. Mixture-Model Tricks exam

Mixtures combine a sum (over components) with a product (over observations). The latent-indicator trick turns this into something Gibbs-friendly.

5.1 Observed-data likelihood

\[ y_i \mid \theta, w \sim \sum_{m=1}^M w_m f_m(y_i\mid \theta_m), \qquad w_m\ge0,\; \sum_{m=1}^M w_m=1. \]
\[ p(y\mid \theta,w)=\prod_{i=1}^n \left(\sum_{m=1}^M w_m f_m(y_i\mid\theta_m)\right). \]

The sum inside the product is why direct computation/sampling is awkward.

5.2 Latent indicator augmentation

Introduce \(z_i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\), or one-hot \(z_{im}=\ind(z_i=m)\), with \(P(z_i=m\mid w)=w_m\). Then

\[ p(y,z\mid\theta,w)=\prod_{i=1}^n \prod_{m=1}^M \left[w_m f_m(y_i\mid\theta_m)\right]^{z_{im}}. \]

This is multiplicative in components and gives simple full conditionals. Integrating/summing out \(z\) recovers the observed-data likelihood.

5.3 Full conditional when a component is empty key exam point

Let \(S_m=\{i:z_{im}=1\}\) and \(n_m=|S_m|\). The conditional for \(\theta_m\) is

\[ \pi(\theta_m\mid \cdots) \propto \left[\prod_{i\in S_m} f_m(y_i\mid\theta_m)\right] \pi(\theta_m\mid\theta_{-m}). \]

If \(n_m=0\), the product is empty (equals 1), so \(\pi(\theta_m\mid \cdots, z_m=0)\propto \pi(\theta_m\mid\theta_{-m})\). The component parameter is drawn from its conditional prior. Therefore that prior conditional must be proper.

5.4 Label switching / non-identifiability

The problem
The likelihood is invariant under permutation of component labels: swapping \((w_1,\theta_1)\) and \((w_2,\theta_2)\) gives the same density for \(y\). MCMC may jump between equivalent modes.
  • Ordering constraint: e.g. \(\mu_1<\cdots<\mu_M\), or order by weights/variances; simple but can distort inference.
  • Informative/non-exchangeable priors: break symmetry if scientifically justified.
  • Post-processing/relabeling: align sampled labels by minimizing a loss over permutations.
  • Inference on label-invariant quantities: predictive density, clustering probabilities after relabeling, etc.

6. Rejection & Importance Sampling exam

These methods are all about ratios: target over proposal. The normalizing constant is often unknown, but ratios/weights save you.

6.1 Rejection sampling

Want samples from target \(f\) (possibly unnormalized \(\tilde f\)). Choose proposal \(g\) with same support and \(K\ge \sup_x f(x)/g(x)\).

\[ \text{Draw } X\sim g,\; U\sim\Unif(0,1). \quad \text{Accept if } U \le \frac{f(X)}{K g(X)}. \]

Acceptance probability is \(1/K\) when \(f,g\) are normalized: \(P(\text{acc})=\int g(x)\frac{f(x)}{Kg(x)}\d x=1/K\). So smaller \(K\) is better.

Correctness proof template proof

For any set \(A\),

\[ \begin{aligned} P(X\in A \mid \text{acc}) &= \frac{P(X\in A,\text{acc})}{P(\text{acc})} \\ &= \frac{\int_A g(x)\frac{f(x)}{Kg(x)}\,\d x}{\int_\mathcal{X} g(x)\frac{f(x)}{Kg(x)}\,\d x} = \frac{\int_A f(x)\,\d x}{\int_\mathcal{X} f(x)\,\d x}=F(A). \end{aligned} \]

The \(g(x)\) cancels and \(K\) cancels. That's the whole proof.

Finding K
Maximize \(h(x)=f(x)/g(x)\) (or \(\tilde f/g\)) by derivative/critical points, boundaries, or monotonicity. Report the maximizing \(x\) and verify it is a maximum if needed.

6.2 Importance sampling

\[ \E_f[h(X)] = \int h(x)\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}g(x)\,\d x \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N w(x_i)h(x_i), \quad x_i\sim g,\; w(x)=\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}. \]

Requirements: \(g(x)>0\) wherever \(f(x)|h(x)|>0\). Choose \(g\) with heavier tails than \(f\) in important regions; otherwise weights have high variance.

6.3 Self-normalized importance sampling

If only \(\tilde f(x)=Z f(x)\) is known,

\[ \tilde w(x)=\frac{\tilde f(x)}{g(x)}, \qquad \E_f[h(X)]\approx \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \tilde w(x_i)h(x_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^N \tilde w(x_i)}. \]

The unknown \(Z\) cancels in the ratio. This is a biased but consistent estimator.

Effective sample size intuition
Highly variable weights mean a few samples dominate. Diagnostic: \(ESS \approx 1/\sum_i \bar w_i^2\) or \(N_{eff}\) formulas used in course. Large weight variance = bad proposal.

7. MCMC, Metropolis-Hastings, Gibbs exam

MCMC constructs a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is the target \(\pi\). The key proof tool is detailed balance; the key practical tools are acceptance ratios and full conditionals.

7.1 Core MCMC conditions

ConditionMeaningWhy it matters
Stationary/invariant\(\pi P=\pi\)If you start in \(\pi\), you stay in \(\pi\)
Detailed balance (reversibility)\(\pi(x)P(x,y)=\pi(y)P(y,x)\)Sufficient for stationarity; easy to verify for MH
IrreducibleCan reach any relevant state from any otherChain explores whole support
AperiodicNo fixed cycleAvoids periodic non-convergence
Ergodic (irreducible+aperiodic+recurrent as needed)Averages converge to expectations under \(\pi\)Justifies Monte Carlo estimates from chain

7.2 Metropolis-Hastings algorithm

\[ \alpha(x,y)=\min\left(1,\; \frac{\pi(y)q(x\mid y)}{\pi(x)q(y\mid x)}\right). \]
  1. Given \(x^{(t)}=x\), propose \(y\sim q(\cdot\mid x)\).
  2. Compute \(\alpha(x,y)\).
  3. Set \(x^{(t+1)}=y\) with prob \(\alpha(x,y)\), else \(x^{(t+1)}=x\).
Constants cancel
\(\pi\) can be unnormalized: \(\pi=\tilde\pi/Z\), so \(\pi(y)/\pi(x)=\tilde\pi(y)/\tilde\pi(x)\). Same for symmetric \(q\): \(q(x\mid y)=q(y\mid x)\) cancels.
Detailed balance proof template proof

For \(y\neq x\), \(P(x,y)=q(y\mid x)\alpha(x,y)\). Need \(\pi(x)q(y\mid x)\alpha(x,y)=\pi(y)q(x\mid y)\alpha(y,x)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \pi(x)q(y\mid x)\alpha(x,y)&=\pi(x)q(y\mid x)\min\left(1,\frac{\pi(y)q(x\mid y)}{\pi(x)q(y\mid x)}\right)\\ &=\min\left(\pi(x)q(y\mid x),\;\pi(y)q(x\mid y)\right). \end{aligned} \]

This is symmetric in \(x,y\), hence equals \(\pi(y)q(x\mid y)\alpha(y,x)\).

7.3 Special cases and proposal tricks

CaseProposalAcceptance ratioNote
Random-walk Metropolis \(q(y\mid x)=q(x\mid y)\) \(\min(1,\pi(y)/\pi(x))\) Step size controls acceptance/mixing
Independence sampler \(q(y\mid x)=g(y)\) \(\min\left(1,\frac{\pi(y)g(x)}{\pi(x)g(y)}\right)\) Proposal should cover target tails
Gibbs sampler \(q(y\mid x)=\pi(y_j\mid x_{-j})\) on component \(j\) \(1\) Special case of MH; needs full conditionals
Log-scale / positive parameters
If \(\theta>0\), proposing on \(\phi=\log\theta\) avoids invalid negative proposals. Either include the Jacobian in the target/proposal logic or use a proposal whose density is written correctly in the same parameterization. Always write the MH ratio in one consistent coordinate system.

7.4 Gibbs sampling

\[ \theta_j^{(t+1)} \sim \pi\!\left(\theta_j \mid \theta_1^{(t+1)},\ldots,\theta_{j-1}^{(t+1)},\theta_{j+1}^{(t)},\ldots,\theta_d^{(t)}, x\right). \]
  • Get each full conditional by taking joint posterior and treating everything except \(\theta_j\) as constant.
  • If conditional is a known kernel, sample directly (no rejection/MH step needed).
  • If not, can use Metropolis-within-Gibbs.
  • Highly correlated parameters cause slow mixing; consider blocking or reparameterization.

7.5 Convergence diagnostics / practical checks

  • Trace plots: look for stationarity and good mixing, not trends/sticking.
  • Autocorrelation: high autocorrelation means less information per draw; ESS decreases.
  • Multiple chains: different starts should converge to same region.
  • Burn-in: discard early transient draws, but burn-in does not fix non-convergence.

8. Decision Theory, Asymptotics & Other Useful Moves

These show up as short-answer/concept questions and occasionally as small derivations.

8.1 Loss functions core

8.2 Asymptotic intuition

  • With regular models and enough data, posterior concentrates near the true parameter; likelihood dominates prior.
  • MLE and posterior mode/mean often become close asymptotically (Bernstein–von Mises gives normal approximation under conditions).
  • Finite \(n\): prior can matter a lot. Large \(n\): posterior inference usually stabilizes, but Bayes factors can still be sensitive to prior width.

8.4 Optimization/viewpoint tricks

  • MAP: \(\hat\theta_{MAP}=\argmax_\theta [\log L(x\mid\theta)+\log\pi(\theta)]\). Prior acts as regularizer/penalty.
  • MLE: MAP with flat prior (when appropriate/proper).
  • Log posterior: convert mode-finding to optimization/sum of terms.

10. Common Pitfalls

Don't forget the parameterization
Gamma(rate \(b\)) vs Gamma(scale), Normal variance vs precision, Inverse-Gamma vs Gamma on precision. A right kernel with wrong parameter mapping loses marks.
Proportionality is not equality
\(\pi(\theta\mid x)\propto g(\theta)\) means equal up to a constant in \(\theta\). Don't write \(=\) unless you've included the normalizing constant.
Support matters
Check \(\theta\in(0,1)\), \(\sigma^2>0\), \(\sum w_m=1\), proposal support covering target support. Many MCMC/integration mistakes are support mistakes.
Improper prior != improper posterior
An improper prior can sometimes yield a proper posterior, but you must argue/integrate. Never assume. Also, improper priors break ordinary Bayes factors.
MH acceptance is not just target ratio
It's \(\min(1,\frac{\pi(y)q(x\mid y)}{\pi(x)q(y\mid x)})\). The reverse proposal term disappears only for symmetric proposals.
Gibbs needs full conditionals
"Draw from full conditional" means conditional on all other parameters and data. If the conditional isn't standard, you need another sampler step (Metropolis-within-Gibbs, slice, etc.).

Final exam micro-checklist

  • Write the object first: posterior, predictive, marginal likelihood, acceptance probability, weight.
  • Drop constants in \(\theta\) early; keep them if integrating over \(\theta\).
  • State kernel name/parameters after matching.
  • For proofs, say what cancels: \(K\), \(g(x)\), normalizing constant \(Z\), arbitrary improper-prior constant \(c\).
  • Sanity check: densities integrate to 1, probabilities in [0,1], variances positive, weights/proportions sum appropriately.
